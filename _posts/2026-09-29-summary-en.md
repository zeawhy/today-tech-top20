The findings show that privacy risks in conversational AI are not limited to model training but extend to everyday ad-tracking infrastructure, affecting anyone who uses these assistants for sensitive or work-related conversations. It also strengthens the argument that users cannot rely on cookie banners or UUID-based URLs to keep their chats private. The study covers both web clients and Android apps, and the leakage includes conversation-derived artifacts rather than just generic telemetry. The persistence of trackers after cookie rejection suggests that consent mechanisms are largely ineffective for these services.

A six-month UK trial of live facial recognition (LFR) cameras deployed at 16 rail stations scanned approximately 500,000 faces but produced no arrests and only one false positive, according to a Guardian report. The trial's results have reignited debate over whether such surveillance systems deliver meaningful public safety value relative to their cost and privacy impact. The outcome is a critical real-world data point in the global debate over live facial recognition, suggesting that large-scale deployment may yield negligible law-enforcement returns while still subjecting hundreds of thousands of innocent people to biometric scanning. It could influence UK policy, police procurement decisions, and similar trials in other countries weighing surveillance against civil liberties. The single false positive means the system wrongly flagged one person as a match, a known risk of facial recognition that can lead to wrongful stops or arrests; accuracy is also known to degrade in uncontrolled surveillance settings and to vary across demographic groups. The scale of scanning was modest — roughly equivalent to two days of footfall at Liverpool Street Station — implying the cameras were active only a small fraction of the time.

**Background**: Live facial recognition uses AI-powered cameras to capture real-time images of faces and compare them against a watchlist database to identify persons of interest. A false positive occurs when the software wrongly considers two different people to be the same person, while a false negative is a failure to match two images of the same person. Research such as the 2018 'Gender Shades' study has shown error rates can vary sharply by demographic, with much higher error rates for darker-skinned women than light-skinned men.

Shopify has expanded its WebMCP support from storefronts and carts to the checkout step, allowing browser-based AI agents to read the checkout screen, update order details such as shipping address or delivery options, and complete a purchase once the buyer authorizes it. Checkout is the most commercially sensitive step in e-commerce, so letting AI agents act there signals that agentic commerce is moving from browsing and cart-building into real transactions, which could reshape how shoppers, retailers, and agent developers interact with online stores. The agent actions still require explicit buyer authorization before an order is finalized, and the capability is built on WebMCP, which exposes page functions as callable tools rather than relying on fragile screen-scraping or simulated clicks.

rss · TechCrunch AI · Sep 28, 19:33

**Background**: WebMCP (Web Model Context Protocol) is an emerging standard that lets web pages act like Model Context Protocol servers, implementing tools in client-side script instead of on a backend. ## [NeurIPS Paper Formalizes Adaptive Representations for Functional Gradient Descent](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wsejb7/functional_gradient_descent_with_adaptive/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new NeurIPS-accepted paper, "Functional Gradient Descent with Adaptive Representations," formalizes a broad class of approximation schemes called adaptive representations that provably converge to the global minimizer of a functional objective. The resulting algorithms outperform corresponding neural networks by up to an order of magnitude across several settings, and the first author is answering questions in the Reddit discussion. Functional gradient descent methods often beat neural networks in theory but are hard to implement correctly because naive approximations converge to the wrong solution. By providing provable convergence guarantees with immediately implementable algorithms, this work could make functional optimization practical and influence both optimization theory and machine learning practice. The core challenge is that functional gradients are infinite-dimensional objects, so they must be approximated in finite-dimensional form; the paper shows that naive approximation leads to convergence at the wrong point, while the proposed adaptive representations guarantee convergence to the global minimizer. The authors note this is only a starting point for the line of work, and the paper is available on arXiv at 2606.16926.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/dccsillag0 · Sep 28, 13:23

**Background**: Functional gradient descent generalizes ordinary gradient descent from parameter vectors to functions, treating the function itself as the object being optimized; it underlies methods like gradient boosting, where each weak learner approximates a gradient direction in function space. Because function spaces are infinite-dimensional, the gradient (a functional derivative) cannot be represented exactly on a computer, so practical algorithms must approximate it. Adaptive representations are schemes that choose or refine the finite-dimensional basis used for this approximation during optimization, rather than fixing it in advance.
