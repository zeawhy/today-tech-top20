## [SemiAnalysis Analyzes GLM-5.3 Sparse Attention and HBM Memory Impact](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/sparse-savings-persistent-demand-inside-glm53) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SemiAnalysis published a deep-dive analysis of GLM-5.3, examining how its sparse attention mechanisms — including KV cache offloading, HiSparse, DeepSeek-style sparse attention, and IndexShare — affect HBM memory usage and DRAM demand. The report frames these optimizations as a shift from dense attention toward sparse, memory-tiered inference for long-context models. Sparse attention and KV cache offloading could substantially reduce the HBM footprint required for long-context inference, which directly affects GPU memory costs and the total addressable market for HBM and DRAM. This matters for anyone deploying or investing in large MoE models, since memory bandwidth and capacity are often the binding constraint on inference economics. GLM-5.3 is described as a 744B-class MoE model extending sparse attention with IndexShare for 1M-token contexts, while the Flash variant is a 320B-total/18B-active multimodal MoE with hybrid KDA and sparse MLA attention, native FP8 weights, MTP, and a 1M-token context window. KV cache offloading moves KV blocks beyond GPU HBM into lower-cost CPU DRAM and shared storage tiers, extending effective cache capacity.

rss · Semianalysis · Sep 28, 19:26

**Background**: HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) is the high-speed memory stacked on GPUs, and it holds model weights, activations, and the KV cache during LLM inference. The KV cache stores key/value tensors for all previous tokens so they can be reused during decoding, and it grows with context length, making it a major consumer of GPU memory. Sparse attention reduces the number of tokens each query attends to, while KV cache offloading moves cached tensors to cheaper memory tiers such as CPU DRAM, both aimed at relieving HBM pressure for long-context and agentic workloads.

## [NeurIPS Paper Formalizes Adaptive Representations for Functional Gradient Descent](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wsejb7/functional_gradient_descent_with_adaptive/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper accepted at NeurIPS, "Functional Gradient Descent with Adaptive Representations," formalizes a broad class of approximation schemes called adaptive representations that provably ensure convergence to the global minimizer of infinite-dimensional functional gradients. The resulting algorithms outperform corresponding neural networks often by an order of magnitude across multiple settings, and the first author is actively answering questions in the Reddit discussion. This work addresses a core practical obstacle in functional gradient descent—the need to approximate infinite-dimensional gradients without converging to the wrong solution—and could make FGD a more reliable alternative to neural networks for optimization and learning tasks. If the order-of-magnitude gains hold, it may influence how practitioners design and implement function-space optimization algorithms. The paper introduces a theoretically grounded FGD algorithm that adapts the representation of functional gradients during optimization, and the authors claim the schemes are immediately implementable while provably converging to global minimizers. The work is described as an early step for this line of research, with the paper available at arXiv:2606.16926.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/dccsillag0 · Sep 28, 13:23

**Background**: Gradient descent is a standard first-order optimization method that iteratively minimizes a differentiable function by moving in the direction of steepest descent. Functional gradient descent (FGD) performs gradient descent directly in function space, which offers strong convergence theory and a clean mathematical framework, but it is difficult to implement because functional gradients are infinite-dimensional and must be approximated. Naive approximations can cause the algorithm to converge to the wrong place, so the new paper formalizes adaptive representation schemes to fix this issue.

## [Qwen3-VL 8B on a laptop beats GPT-5.6 on tax forms but fails on Indian dates](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wsbqni/qwen3vl_8b_on_a_laptop_vs_opus_55_sonnet_5_gpt56/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A practitioner benchmarked Qwen3-VL 8B Instruct (Q4_K_M via Ollama on an M5 24GB laptop, ~30s/doc) against Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5, and GPT-5.6 Terra across 137 messy documents including CORD/SROIE receipts, scanned 1980s-90s invoices, 32 real IRS forms, synthetic Indian bank statements, and CUAD contracts. Qwen 8B scored 59% fully-correct documents versus Opus 89%, Sonnet 85%, and GPT-5.6 Terra 57%, notably winning on W-2s (21/32 vs 7/32) but failing on Indian bank statements (2/10) and long contracts (2/15). The results show that a small, locally-run 8B vision-language model can already outperform a frontier proprietary model on structured form extraction like tax documents, which matters for privacy-sensitive and cost-constrained document processing workflows. At the same time, the failures on date formats and long contracts highlight that local models still need task-specific fine-tuning before they can replace frontier APIs in production. The default qwen3-vl:8b tag in Ollama is the thinking variant and ignores think:false, causing it to burn all 4,096 tokens on reasoning and return nothing on long contracts; users should use the :8b-instruct tag instead. The benchmark also found that GPT-5.6 Terra silently "corrects" unusual spellings (Rachael→Rachel, Kelleyland→Kellyland), self-checking prompts changed almost nothing (119/137 identical outputs), and at least 4 of the 30 SROIE receipts have wrong published answer keys.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/NegotiationKey7184 · Sep 28, 11:11

**Background**: Vision-language models (VLMs) like Qwen3-VL can read both images and text, making them useful for extracting structured data from scanned documents such as receipts, invoices, and tax forms. Findings from the Zhurong rover, including evidence of a continent-scale ancient ocean about 3.5 billion years ago and possible short-lived flooding around 1.6 billion years ago, will be incorporated into the map. Alongside the global map, a 1:50,000 geological map of the landing area and a series of specialized thematic atlases will be compiled, and the results are to be released as the world's first intelligent Mars geological map. The project is a planned effort rather than a completed result, with completion targeted for the end of 2028.

OpenAI says it did not learn of the breach until August, even though the agent's access reportedly occurred in June, and it notified the government via a single email to a generic Services Australia inbox despite recent meetings between company executives and Australian officials.
