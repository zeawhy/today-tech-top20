## [NASA/ESA Mars Sample Return Mission Cancelled](https://www.science.org/content/article/nasa-s-mars-sample-return-mission-dead) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The NASA/ESA Mars Sample Return (MSR) mission has been cancelled, ending the multi-mission campaign that was approved in 2022 to retrieve rock and soil samples collected by NASA's Perseverance rover in Jezero crater. The cancellation also eliminates the planned Sample Receiving Facility that would have contained the samples upon their arrival on Earth. This is a major setback for planetary science, as returned Martian samples could have been analyzed on Earth with far more powerful instruments than any rover can carry, particularly to search for potential biosignatures. It also shifts the landscape of international space exploration, leaving China's Tianwen-3 as the leading planned Mars sample-return effort. Perseverance has brought 43 tubes to Mars, 38 for samples and five 'witness tubes' to document sampling-system cleanliness, and it has already deposited a depot of 10 backup sample tubes. The cancellation was driven by the US Congress in January 2026, and no replacement retrieval mission is currently planned by NASA or ESA.

hackernews · Muhammad523 · Sep 21, 19:14 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49791939)

**Background**: A Mars sample-return mission is a proposed effort to collect rock and dust on Mars and bring them back to Earth, enabling far more extensive analysis than onboard rover sensors, especially regarding whether Mars once hosted life. NASA and ESA's MSR campaign was approved in 2022 to retrieve samples gathered by Perseverance, which has been caching them on the Martian surface. Concerns about possible back contamination of Earth's biosphere from returned samples have been raised, though the risk is generally considered low.

## [Bryan Cantrill Reflects on What Sun Microsystems Got Wrong](https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/20/what-sun-got-wrong/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Bryan Cantrill, a former Sun Microsystems engineer and co-creator of DTrace, published a retrospective blog post titled "What Sun got wrong" analyzing the strategic and technical mistakes that led to the company's decline. The post sparked a large Hacker News discussion with 427 points and 241 comments, where industry veterans shared firsthand accounts of Sun's sales practices and strategic missteps. Sun's collapse remains one of the most instructive case studies in tech history, showing how a dominant vertically integrated vendor can be disrupted by commodity x86 hardware and open-source Linux. The discussion offers lessons for today's tech industry, with commenters drawing parallels to current high-valuation companies like Tesla and SpaceX. Commenters highlighted specific missteps: Sun briefly cancelled Solaris on x86 in 2002, failed to strike a deal with Google in 2002 over server-count secrecy, and lacked a credible onboarding path for new clients who preferred developing on x86 with Linux. One commenter noted Sun's stock fell from $70 at the dot-com peak to $7 within months, while another recalled that Sun and DEC's sales process—requiring live meetings and endless quote revisions—was far more burdensome than Dell's.

**Background**: Sun Microsystems was an American technology company that existed from 1982 to 2010, developing and selling computers, servers, and the Solaris operating system, and was known for its SPARC processor architecture. Bryan Cantrill worked at Sun and later Oracle after its acquisition of Sun, and co-developed DTrace, a dynamic tracing framework for production systems. Sun's stock lost 51% of its value in 2001 and another 76% in 2002, and the company was eventually acquired by Oracle in 2010.

## [Cloudflare Python Workers reach general availability](https://blog.cloudflare.com/python-workers-ga/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Cloudflare announced the general availability of Python Workers, allowing developers to run Python code inside its WebAssembly-based serverless edge runtime with improved package support. The GA release builds on the earlier preview and adds standardized packaging via PEP 783 (PyEmscripten) and upstream contributions that let HTTP clients like Requests route through the JavaScript fetch API. This is a significant milestone for serverless Python, since Python is one of the most popular languages but has historically been hard to run efficiently in edge/serverless environments. It gives Python developers a first-class path to Cloudflare's global edge network, potentially reshaping how Python web apps and APIs are deployed. The runtime is built on Pyodide, a CPython port to WebAssembly/Emscripten, and package support is now standardized through PEP 783. Community members noted that cold-start performance and some architectural constraints remain open questions, and that upstream work on urllib3's Pyodide/Emscripten and JSPI support was largely done by external contributors.

hackernews · torutofu · Sep 21, 13:38 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49787142)

**Background**: Cloudflare Workers is a serverless platform launched in 2017 that runs code on Cloudflare's edge network without requiring developers to manage infrastructure. Python Workers execute Python inside WebAssembly, a portable binary format originally designed for the browser, rather than as native code. Pyodide is an open-source project that ports CPython to WebAssembly, enabling Python packages such as NumPy and pandas to run in these constrained environments.

## [Apple Unveils M6, First 2nm Chip, and Quad-Die M5 Ultra](https://t.me/zaihuapd/43965) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Apple announced the M6, its first chip built on a 2nm process, debuting in a new Mac mini with a 12-core CPU, 12-core GPU, dual 16-core neural engines, and up to 170GB/s unified memory bandwidth. Alongside it, Apple introduced the M5 Ultra in a new Mac Studio, its first quad-die M-series chip, offering up to 36 CPU cores, 80 GPU cores, 512GB of memory, and 1.2TB/s of memory bandwidth. The M6 marks Apple's transition to the 2nm node, which should bring meaningful performance-per-watt gains and keep its silicon ahead of competitors in laptops and desktops. The M5 Ultra's quad-die design pushes Apple's high-end desktop performance further, directly targeting professional workloads like 3D rendering, video production, and local AI inference. The M5 Ultra's 1.2TB/s memory bandwidth is 50% higher than the M3 Ultra, making it Apple's most powerful chip to date. The M6's 2nm process is expected to deliver better performance or lower power consumption compared to the previous 3nm generation, though actual device benchmarks are not yet available.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 21, 16:32

**Background**: The "2nm process" refers to a semiconductor manufacturing node that succeeds 3nm, where the number is a marketing term rather than a physical measurement; it typically enables higher transistor density and better energy efficiency. Apple's M-series chips use a unified memory architecture, meaning the CPU, GPU, and neural engines share the same memory pool, which reduces data copying and latency. The M5 Ultra's quad-die design connects four silicon dies using Apple's UltraFusion packaging technology, effectively combining two dual-die M5 Max chips into one system on a chip.
