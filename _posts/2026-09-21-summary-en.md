## [Terry Tao asks whether human mathematicians are still needed in the AI era](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/19/why-do-we-need-human-mathematicians-anymore/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Terence Tao, a Fields Medalist and professor of mathematics at UCLA, published a blog post titled "Why do we need human mathematicians anymore?" in which he examines whether human mathematicians remain necessary as AI systems become increasingly capable at theorem proving and mathematical discovery. The post quickly became a focal point of discussion, drawing 216 points and 203 comments on Hacker News. The question strikes at the heart of how the mathematical community, and knowledge work more broadly, should adapt as AI automates more cognitive tasks. Tao's prominence means his framing of the issue will likely shape how mathematicians, funding bodies, and AI researchers think about the division of labor between humans and machines. The post is framed as a philosophical reflection rather than a technical result, and the discussion it sparked ranges across intuition versus formal notation, the accessibility of mathematical institutions, and whether mathematics has a finite set of problems to solve. Tao has separately discussed AI and the purpose of doing mathematics at the 2026 International Congress of Mathematicians.

**Background**: Terence Tao is one of the world's most renowned living mathematicians, known for work spanning analysis, number theory, and combinatorics, and he has been an active commentator on AI's growing role in mathematics. Automated theorem proving is a long-established subfield in which computer programs attempt to prove mathematical statements, and recent AI systems have begun producing novel results, including a theorem in algebraic geometry that the president of the American Mathematical Society called "rigorous, correct, and elegant." These advances have prompted debates about proof abundance, verification, and what human understanding adds beyond correct output.

## [Qwen Image 2.1: 7B Open-Weight Text-to-Image Model with Native Transparency](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen-image-2.1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Qwen has released Qwen Image 2.1, a 7B open-weight text-to-image and image editing model that is significantly smaller than its 20B predecessor, Qwen-Image 1. It introduces native transparency (RGBA output) and state-of-the-art text rendering, and is natively supported in ComfyUI on Day 0. The model's smaller size makes high-quality local image generation more accessible, while its native transparency and superior text rendering fill gaps that most open-weight competitors have not addressed. However, its more restrictive license compared to previous Apache-licensed Qwen models may limit commercial adoption. The generation transformer uses 7B parameters across 32 single-stream DiT layers and natively produces transparent RGBA images, a capability that Qwen's team appears to be uniquely tackling among open-weight models. The model is available on Hugging Face and GitHub, with ComfyUI workflows provided for both text-to-image and image editing.

hackernews · jmillikin · Sep 20, 13:09 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49775499)

**Background**: Text-to-image models generate images from text prompts, and open-weight models allow users to run them locally and inspect or modify their weights. Qwen is Alibaba's AI model family, and previous Qwen image models used permissive Apache 2.0 licenses. Native transparency means the model outputs images with an alpha channel directly, avoiding the need for separate background removal tools.

The Snowden archive reshaped global debates on mass surveillance, privacy, and the balance between national security and a free press, so understanding why most of it remains unpublished matters for journalism, whistleblowing policy, and future disclosures. ## [The LLMentalist Effect: Is LLM Intelligence Just Cold Reading?](https://softwarecrisis.dev/letters/llmentalist/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A 2023 article on softwarecrisis.dev argues that the perceived intelligence of chat-based large language models is an illusion, comparing their behavior to the cold reading techniques used by psychics and mediums. The piece sparked a 272-comment Hacker News discussion debating whether LLMs are truly intelligent or merely mimic understanding. This analogy challenges the widespread assumption that fluent chatbot responses demonstrate genuine reasoning or understanding, and it pushes the AI community to scrutinize how much of LLM behavior is statistically generic rather than truly insightful. The debate touches on the Turing test, anthropomorphism, and the criteria we use to define intelligence, affecting researchers, developers, and users who interact with these systems daily. The article specifically targets chat-based LLMs, noting that techniques like validation statements and the Forer effect let both chatbots and psychics appear to give extremely specific answers that are actually statistically generic. It also remarks that AI research has a reputation for disregarding other fields, suggesting the reimplementation of a psychic's con was likely accidental.

hackernews · jalev · Sep 20, 12:20 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49775104)

**Background**: Cold reading is a technique used by phony psychics to subtly extract information from a client without them realizing it, often through high-probability guesses and quick reactions to signals. The Forer effect, also known as the Barnum effect, is the tendency for people to rate vague, generic personality descriptions as highly accurate for themselves. The article argues that chat-based LLMs exploit these same psychological mechanisms, creating an illusion of intelligence that mirrors a psychic's con.

## [Moonshot AI in talks with Microsoft, Amazon, Google over Kimi K3 revenue share](https://t.me/zaihuapd/43950) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Moonshot AI is reportedly negotiating revenue-sharing agreements with Microsoft, Amazon, and Google for its Kimi K3 model, initially seeking up to a 30% cut of revenue. If concluded, it would be the first major revenue-sharing deal between a Chinese AI company and US cloud giants, though talks remain at an early stage with key terms undecided and all parties declining to comment. A successful deal would mark the first large-scale revenue-sharing arrangement between a Chinese AI firm and US hyperscalers, potentially reshaping how frontier models are distributed and monetized globally. It also signals that even amid US-China tech tensions, American cloud providers may be willing to host and sell access to leading Chinese open-weight models. Kimi K3 was released in July 2026 with 2.8 trillion total parameters, making it the largest open-weight model ever and the first open-source 3T-class model; it features native multimodality and a 1M-token context window. Moonshot's annual recurring revenue reportedly surpassed $300 million by mid-June, and the 30% figure is described as an initial ask rather than a settled term.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 21, 06:44

**Background**: Moonshot AI is a Chinese AI startup behind the Kimi series of open-weight large language models, which are distributed via web, API, and the Kimi Code command-line agent. Revenue-sharing deals are a common cloud-industry arrangement in which a model developer receives a percentage of the money a cloud provider earns from selling access to that model. Kimi K3's predecessor K2 was a trillion-parameter open-source model that competed with DeepSeek-V3, and K3 extends that lineage to 2.8 trillion parameters.
