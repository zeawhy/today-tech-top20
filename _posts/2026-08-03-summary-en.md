## [OpenAI's Astra Model Achieves Breakthroughs on Ten Long-Standing Math Problems](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The company claims the token cost for generating each argument was under $2,000 at GPT-5.6 Sol token prices. This marks a significant milestone in AI-assisted mathematical research, potentially accelerating discovery in fields that have seen little progress for decades. The proofs were formalized in Lean 4, and the results are available in the openai/ten-proofs GitHub repository, along with a paper and an LLM-generated PDF that reconstructs the reasoning process. ## [Kakehashi: Userspace Layer Runs macOS Binaries on Linux ARM](https://github.com/wie-project/kakehashi) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Kakehashi, an experimental userspace translation layer, successfully runs macOS ARM64 CLI binaries on Linux aarch64 without a JIT. Working prototypes include 7-Zip, curl, and Xcode Tools Git, with 7-Zip passing multi-threaded compression tests and curl passing over 200 commands. This project addresses a significant technical challenge in binary compatibility, potentially enabling macOS CLI tools to run natively on Linux ARM hardware. It could reduce reliance on emulation or virtualization, benefiting developers and users who need macOS-specific tools on ARM Linux systems. Kakehashi loads Darwin Mach-O binaries on Linux aarch64, maps a freestanding libSystem, and translates BSD syscalls. Currently, 7-Zip is about 5.2x slower than native Linux execution, but the developer has an optimization plan to reduce this gap.

hackernews · vlad_kalinkin · Aug 2, 16:26 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49145937)

**Background**: Binary compatibility allows software compiled for one OS or architecture to run on another without modification. Projects like Wine and Darling have explored similar goals for Windows and macOS applications on Linux, but Kakehashi focuses specifically on ARM64 and CLI tools, using a userspace approach without a JIT.

## [Tech Giants Sign Open Letter Opposing Open-Weight AI Restrictions](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/2/open-letters/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Microsoft-led open letter, 'Open Weights and American AI Leadership,' dated July 24, 2026, was signed by 235 AI-related companies including NVIDIA, Amazon, Y Combinator, The Linux Foundation, and later OpenAI, opposing restrictions on open-weight models. A subsequent letter, 'Pacing the Frontier,' signed by 1,324 employees of frontier AI companies, called for international efforts to pace automated AI development. This marks a significant industry alignment against potential US government restrictions on open-weight models, highlighting the tension between AI safety concerns and innovation. The involvement of major players like Microsoft, NVIDIA, and OpenAI signals broad impact on AI policy and the future of open-weight model development. The Microsoft-led letter notably supports distillation, a technique where models train on outputs from other models, urging policymakers not to conflate it with misappropriation. Anthropic did not sign the letter and instead published its own position three days later, with CEO Dario Amodei calling for a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation while stating Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 2, 04:16

**Background**: Open-weight models are AI systems whose learned parameters (weights and biases) are publicly released, allowing others to download, use, and sometimes modify them. This contrasts with closed models, which are kept proprietary. The debate centers on balancing the benefits of open access and innovation against potential risks such as misuse for cyberattacks or biological attacks, and concerns about authoritarian governments developing powerful AI.

The maintainer of KataGo published a study examining how superhuman Go neural networks internally represent board symmetries, finding that they learn orientation-independent concepts to a significant degree despite only stochastic data augmentation. The study includes unexpected findings and is accompanied by code and an educational writeup. This research contributes to interpretability in deep learning, specifically how neural networks handle symmetries in input space. It provides insights into the internal representations of a state-of-the-art Go AI, which could inform future model design and training strategies for board games and other domains with inherent symmetries. The study focuses on KataGo, an open-source Go program using ResNet architectures with pre-activation, and uses stochastic 8-fold data augmentation during training. The writeup is noted to be largely AI-generated with human direction, and the code is linked from the post.

**Background**: Go is a board game with complete symmetry under rotation and reflection, but neural networks for Go are not explicitly designed to enforce this symmetry. Instead, they rely on stochastic data augmentation, where each training batch is randomly oriented, to encourage the model to learn orientation-invariant features. This study investigates how well such networks achieve internal symmetry, using interpretability methods to analyze the learned representations.

EA's financial data will no longer be publicly disclosed after the deal closes.
