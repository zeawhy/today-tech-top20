## [Chinese Scientists Build World's First Nuclear Clock](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11122-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A research team at Tsinghua University has developed the world's first nuclear clock, using a self-built 148 nm continuous-wave vacuum ultraviolet laser and a thorium-229 doped calcium fluoride crystal, and reported stable operation in Nature. This is the first successful nuclear clock, marking a paradigm shift in timekeeping that could enable a new generation of frequency standards for satellite navigation, deep-space exploration, and tests of fundamental physics. The clock uses the low-energy isomeric transition of thorium-229 as its reference frequency, addressed by a 148 nm vacuum ultraviolet laser; the solid-state doped-crystal approach is notable because it avoids the complexity of trapped-ion systems, though the clock is not yet competitive with existing atomic clocks.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 8, 05:19

**Background**: Conventional atomic clocks measure time by monitoring the resonant frequency of electron transitions in atoms such as cesium or strontium. A nuclear clock instead uses photons from a nuclear isomeric transition, which is far less sensitive to external perturbations and could offer higher accuracy. Thorium-229 is uniquely suited for this because its isomer has an exceptionally low excitation energy, making it accessible to resonant laser excitation at around 148 nm in the vacuum ultraviolet region.
