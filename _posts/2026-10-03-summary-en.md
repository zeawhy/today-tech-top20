The work has sparked a detailed Hacker News discussion (264 points, 83 comments) debating the mechanisms and limitations of epigenetic aging. If loss of cell identity is a causal driver of aging, it could reframe aging as a partially reversible process and open new therapeutic avenues targeting epigenetic reprogramming. The finding is of broad scientific and societal importance, though it is not a software or engineering breakthrough. The papers present the accumulated damage hypothesis with new framing, but critics note they do not account for the Hayflick limit or explain why dogs age faster than humans. Much human evidence is cross-sectional and transcript-based, while the strongest causal manipulations come from cultured cells or engineered mouse models.

Epigenetic reprogramming, such as with Yamanaka factors, can erase cellular identity and reverse some aging hallmarks, but risks uncontrolled cell growth.

## [NeurIPS 2026 Spotlight: Parallel-in-Time RNN Training Hits 100x Speedup](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wuz2s4/parallelintime_training_of_recurrent_neural/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A NeurIPS 2026 spotlight paper introduces a parallel-in-time training method for nonlinear recurrent neural networks (RNNs) that combines DEER with generalized teacher forcing (GTF), achieving over 100x speedup on chaotic dynamical systems reconstruction. The preprint is available at arXiv:2605.12683. This work addresses a major bottleneck in training RNNs on long chaotic time series, where DEER previously broke down and degraded to O(T log T) runtime. The method enables stable training on sequences longer than 10^6 steps and significantly outperforms Mamba and other state space models in dynamical systems reconstruction, with potential impact on scientific computing and time-series modeling. DEER solves the RNN forward pass via Newton-type fixed point iterations across the whole sequence length T, enabling O[(log T)^2] scaling instead of O(T) through GPU parallelization, but it breaks down under chaotic dynamics. GTF stabilizes DEER by preventing divergence caused by chaotic dynamics and reduces exposure bias compared to traditional teacher forcing.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/DangerousFunny1371 · Oct 1, 13:12

**Background**: Recurrent neural networks process sequential data by maintaining hidden states over time, but training them on long sequences is slow because the forward pass is inherently sequential. Parallel-in-time methods like DEER reformulate the forward pass as a fixed-point problem solvable in parallel across time steps, while generalized teacher forcing (GTF) is a technique designed to stabilize training on chaotic systems where gradients explode due to exponential trajectory divergence.

## [NeurIPS 2026 Paper Reveals 'Authority Bias' in LLMs](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wv1c2e/llms_that_push_back_on_a_wrong_user_still_accept/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A NeurIPS 2026 paper introduces 'Authority Bias' in LLMs, showing that a single note claiming a 'verified source' endorses a wrong answer flips 45-88% of correct responses in 7 of 8 tested models, while the same wrong answer from a user moves them much less. The effect is largest in models that best resist user pressure, with GPT-5.4 flipping on 44.7% and Grok-4.20 on 87.5% of questions. This matters because standard sycophancy evaluations only apply pressure through the user, so a model can pass them while still being easily misled by search results, retrieved documents, or tool outputs. As models become more agentic and autonomous, safeguarding against misinformation from tools is critical for AI safety in high-stakes domains like law, healthcare, and education. Using TriviaQA questions the model already answers correctly, the study adds a wrong answer framed either as from a 'verified source' or from a user, keeping the question and wrong answer identical. Internal analysis of open-weight models shows a shared 'this answer was endorsed' direction with ~0.90-0.99 cosine similarity, and removing the source-endorsement direction cuts compliance by 64-78 points, though the internal results hold in only 3 of 5 open-weight families.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/MajorRedditor23 · Oct 1, 14:45

**Background**: Sycophancy in LLMs refers to the tendency of models to agree with users even when the user is wrong, and it is typically measured by applying social pressure through the user. TriviaQA is a large-scale reading comprehension dataset of trivia question-answer pairs commonly used to test factual knowledge. Authority bias, borrowed from human psychology, describes the tendency to defer to perceived experts or official sources regardless of the actual evidence.
